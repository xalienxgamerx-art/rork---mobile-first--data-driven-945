# Emberfall — Master Development Roadmap & Creative Direction

*Owner's master document, 2026-11-10. The app keeps the name **Greenhollow**; Emberfall is the project codename. This file is the project's authoritative roadmap copy.*

## Creative direction

The vision is unchanged at heart: an open-world fantasy RPG and living-world simulation where the world would carry on if the player never arrived. Inhabitants have their own routines, ambitions, relationships, problems, and histories; systems interact without requiring player involvement.

Six inspirations shape the creative direction — as design references, never templates to reproduce:

- **Fable** — atmosphere, personality, whimsy with occasional darkness.
- **Dragon Quest** — warmth and welcoming charm.
- **The Bard's Tale** — humour and irreverence.
- **Skyrim** — depth, exploration, the pull of the next hill.
- **Oblivion** — systemic schedules and a simulated populace.
- **The Sims** — needs-driven autonomy.

The game keeps its own identity, history, setting, visual language, and mechanics.

### Phase 3 — Living-world simulation <status>Milestones 1–5 implemented (Milestone 2 stabilization-passed); device validation outstanding.</status>

Make the inhabitants independent participants in the world. Five milestones:

- **Milestone 1 — Autonomous NPCs, daily schedules & needs.** *Implemented.* Persistent needs (hunger, fatigue/rest, social, safety), data-driven schedule templates per occupation, priority decisions (danger > critical needs > schedule > routine), three simulation tiers with bounded off-screen catch-up, travel watchdog, save format v3 with v2→v3 migration, world-simulation diagnostics, dialogue conditions for activity and need, 56 passing JVM tests, debug and release builds green.
- **Milestone 2 — Relationships, memory, and social behaviour.** *Implemented, then stabilization-passed.* Directional eight-dimension relationships keyed "holder>other" (familiarity, friendship, trust, respect, affection, fear, resentment, hostility), grown by personality-scaled rules per event type; structured memory records with pure-function fading, protected significant memories, a per-person cap and duplicate suppression; NPC–NPC encounters (conversation, quarrel, shared work) with per-pair cooldowns; gossip as travelling stories — each holder's confidence and distortion per hop, deterministic wording tiers, original event always identifiable; the player participates under id "player" and only learns what they are told, with the reliability shown in the journal; dialogue conditions rel, rel_memory and gossip (with a core pack example); new journal surfaces (village talk, your standing, shared history); save format v4 with a v3→v4 migration that turns old bonds and affinity into real relationship records; social-world diagnostics in Settings and the shared report.
  *Stabilization pass (2026-11-11), all verified by tests:* significant witnessed events (rescues, quarrels, bravery, betrayals — anything weighty enough) now start their stories on the witness's lips automatically, through the production event path, deduped per event and traceable to the witness's own memory; the per-person memory cap is now a hard bound — a life of only great deeds consolidates its two oldest memories of a kind into one condensed entry rather than exceed twenty; chronicle milestones (first meeting, fast friends, falling out, enemies, reconciliation, quiet grudge, and the player-friend line) are noted once per pair and persist in the save, with the keys canonicalised on load like every other opinion; the eight-dimension social world is now the one authoritative ledger — dialogue greetings and rivals, the journal's number, prices and access checks all read a mirror derived from it, which old saves have backfilled on load, so nothing can disagree; and a distant life's workmate and companion are chosen from a small workplace index and the opinions already held, instead of scanning the whole village (which had also been manufacturing blank opinions of everyone). 104 passing JVM tests (21 new), debug and release builds green. Device behaviour unverified.
- **Milestone 3 — Households and family.** *Implemented.* Households and families are two systems, not one: a household is who shares a roof and its larder, kinship is who is bound to whom, and neither implies the other. Kinship is stored canonically — one record per bond, reciprocals derived, symmetric bonds kept as the sorted pair, a save's bond written the derived way round flipped on load, unknown words and strangers reported and let go, unknown ancestry simply no record — and bonds seed the social ledger directly, so family start out knowing and trusting one another with no memory or gossip manufactured of it. Households are data-driven (pack-defined, instantiated into the save at bind, membership the single source of truth behind a lazily rebuilt index) and self-heal on load (unknown members canonicalised with a report, double claims resolved to the first roof, non-member heads cleared, emptied households kept with their history, anyone roofless gaining a shared-residence household with whoever shares their home), and share domestic duties deterministically by character — the generous and loyal step up, the greedy hang back, children only light work, infants none. There is no household clock: cooking fills the larder, housework mends the house, eating drains it (a thin larder makes a thin meal), all through the members' own actions in the existing schedule and activity machinery — cook, housework, care (which warms and steadies the little ones) and visit (to a partner's or kin's roof) are new activities, with free hours spent on the household's waiting work before a wish. Wishes (personal goals) are pack-seeded or grown from character at most one apiece, pursued in the decision ladder behind danger, needs, schedule and duty, advanced abstractly from the actions a life already takes at rates scaled by priority, and completing one is written to the chronicle and remembered by the household — weighty enough to become a story. Children are read from the ages they already have: no patrol or shopping (both become play near home), no wage, care and safety from their carers, a child's routine in the core pack. The journal shows only what the player could have heard: a household appears once the player knows a member (or is kin), kin are named only for kin the player also knows, wishes only a friend shares; new surfaces are a Households page (members, duties, larder, state of the house, history) and family, household and ambitions on each person's page. Dialogue conditions gained kin and goal, and the core pack gained a family — a smith's household with his daughter, a wife keeping her own rooms at the inn, an aunt and nephew farming together. Save stays version 4: every new field decodes from older saves with defaults and is bound on load. Households-and-families diagnostics (roofs kept, moves in and out, work shared out, wishes open and achieved) in Settings and the shared report. 138 passing JVM tests (34 new, including a whole-family integration run through the production pathways and a determinism replay), debug and release builds green. Device behaviour unverified.

**Interlude — dialogue voice (between milestones 2 and 3).** *Implemented.* Every spoken line is voiced in old-school RPG blips, ported from the project's own vocalizer design: each letter always sings the same pentatonic note, vowels carry formant-filtered tones, plosives click, fricatives hiss, questions lift at the end. Four voices (sprite, hero, giant, robot) chosen from the person, not a dice roll: a race may declare a voice for all its folk in its pack definition (the Ashen Elves chirp, the Stoneborn rumble; an unknown word is reported and falls back to age — the young are sprites, the old giants), while character shapes the sound — bullies grind like robots, the fearful chirp, courage lifts the pitch, aggression weighs it down, the sociable talk faster — plus a small per-person pitch so no two villagers sound alike. A Settings › Sound toggle turns voices off. Synthesis and the voice chooser are pure Kotlin, played through an AudioTrack that cancels the previous line and silences on dismiss. A fix pass (2026-11-11): the triangle wave formula was corrected, the per-person pitch now survives negative id hashes, the audio buffer is written in full (partial writes were previously dropped in silence), and song tempo, note lengths and frequencies are clamped into sane bounds — each covered by tests. 104 tests now run and debug and release builds are green. The sound has not been heard on a device.
- **Milestone 4 — Professions and economy.** *Implemented (2026-11-13).* The coin side of the lives the earlier milestones lead: every trade keeps a till staked from its operator's own purse (never minted), wages are paid by real payers from real tills — a till that cannot raise a wage falls into arrears and repays its payroll when trade recovers, rather than creating coin — and rents are charged once a day to whoever holds the paper. Households keep a shared purse their working members contribute to by generosity, and a wealth reading is derived, never stored: means, income and obligations per person, classified into tiers the player can see. Coin is never created or destroyed by a transfer, and the tests account for every one. Save format v7 with a v6→v7 migration (tills, purses and books begin empty); economy diagnostics in Settings and the shared report. 301 passing JVM tests (21 new), debug and release builds green. Device behaviour unverified.
- **Milestone 5 — Settlement simulation.** *Implemented (2026-11-14).* The village itself becomes a subject: a persistent community whose condition emerges each day from the records the other systems already keep — its people (who is housed, young, seeking work or roofless), the trades kept and the state of their tills, the larders, the goods on the shelves, the services a settlement of its kind should have. What cannot be reconstructed is what the settlement owns: its standing (reputation, moved a step a day toward what the place has become, jumped immediately for reasons that are remembered), its open and past events (arrivals, departures, trades opening and closing, thin larders and their recovery, the autumn harvest once a year), a smoothed prosperity reading weighted so no rich man makes a village, and condition hysteresis — growth, decline, housing and hardship become the settlement's truth only after holding several days. The books are kept once a day, day-keyed and bounded (a year away settles as thirty quiet days), with no second clock; people belong to settlements through their household's roof, so a roof that moves moves its people between settlements. Settled in the content packs as anchored places; a world no pack settles stays inert. Save format v8 with a v7→v8 migration (settlement books begin empty); settlement books in Settings and the shared report. 323 passing JVM tests (22 new), debug and release builds green. Device behaviour unverified.

### Phase 4 — Player lifestyle <status>In progress — Milestones 1 and 2 implemented (2026-11-14 / 2026-11-24); device validation outstanding.</status>

The player's own life in the world — living in it, not just adventuring through it. The program makes the player a participant in the same world systems that govern the villagers — the same properties, the same economy, the same settlement books — through the existing player-aware records, with two standing rules: no duplicate player versions of systems that already exist, and the player is never forced through the villagers' autonomous decision-making. The player is never counted as an NPC resident or household member in the settlement books.

- **Milestone 1 — Player integration foundation.** *Implemented (2026-11-14).* An audit of every system's treatment of the player id found much already shared — the property paper is fully id-generic (a player can own, rent, lease, reside, keep a trade and employ today), purchases route coin through the economy's tills, and conversation, gifts, fair trades and rescues already move the villagers' opinions — and closed the real gaps. The player's money now has the same shape as anyone's: savings set aside, rent arrears that become debt (an obligation, never a wealth), a book of accounts kept like a villager's and trimmed from the front, and derived readings — means (purse, savings, their own till, less what they owe), what they can afford, whether they can carry a rent. The daily books collect the player's rent from their purse and then their savings, the shortfall becoming debt — charged once a day however often the books are asked. A trade the player keeps is staked from their own purse when it is new (half their loose coin, capped, moved and never minted), and a trade taken over keeps its till, float and all, following the paper's keeper. Coin passed by transfer is written in both books, the player's included. The player keeps a roof through the same residence and tenancy records as anyone, and the settlement that roof stands in is reported — while the population counts remain untouched by them. No pack villager may take the player's name: the definition is refused with a report. Settings › Diagnostics gained a Player participation section (roof, settlement, paper held, means and debt). Save stays version 8: every new field decodes from older saves with defaults. 337 passing JVM tests (14 new, covering ownership through debt, coin conservation, till staking, save round-trip, the settlement books' blindness to the player, and the impostor guard), debug (24.0 MB) and release (16.2 MB) builds green. Device behaviour unverified. Wage settlement for a player who works for a living remains for the wages milestone — the employment paper already carries their name.
- **Milestone 2 — Player housing & property.** *Implemented (2026-11-24).* The player keeps a home through the villagers' own paper — no second, player-only housing architecture. Every residential, hospitality or mixed-use building carries a derived asking price: a declared price when the paper or pack names one, otherwise a month of its rent, otherwise a plain 100-coin default for a home with no paper at all — seeded onto the property the first time it is asked. The player buys freeholds and takes tenancies through the economy's own books: the purse pays first and savings make up the rest; an unclaimed home's price goes to the settlement's common coffer and an owned one to the seller's pocket; a seller whose roof the player already rents ends the tenancy and returns the sitting deposit while still the landlord of record. Tenancies cost a six-day deposit, returned as far as the landlord can pay when the roof is given up — the common coffer included, for unclaimed paper is the settlement's own rent (whose charge previously vanished; fixed). Buying does not move anyone in: residence is a separate act through the shared move-in records, ending the roof kept elsewhere without touching the deeds, and giving a roof up leaves the other occupants be. The Journal gained a Home page — the player's roof with tenure, settlement, rent, debt and deeds, and the homes of the vale with honest statuses (for sale, to let, the player's own) and buttons for what the paper allows. NPC-owned homes are never put up for sale; construction and furnishing stay out of scope. Saves stay version 8 (price and coffer default to zero; coffers sanitize non-negative); the diagnostics report gained the coffers and the player's paper. 362 passing JVM tests (25 new, accounting for every coin), debug and release builds green on the build runner. Device behaviour unverified.

- **Interlude — glTF 2.0 Binary (.glb) asset pipeline.** *Implemented (2026-11-24).* The engine can now load real 3D assets: a from-scratch, dependency-free glTF 2.0 core (strict .glb container parsing, accessor reading, matrix decomposition) feeds a conversion layer that turns nodes and meshes into the renderer's own vertex format (position/normal/colour, PBR base-colour factors baked into vertices), with static meshes baked once and skinned meshes CPU-skinned per frame into a dynamic buffer (linear blend skinning, four weights per joint, a 20,000-vertex budget above which a mesh falls back to its bind pose). Skinned actors run animation clips sampled from the engine's own clock — a clip ladder with `interrupt` crossfading replaces the old hardcoded pose flags, timings still drawn from existing state so feet stay in sync with the ground — and pack content declares models as ordinary data (`models` table, pack-local refs canonicalised, per-actor model handles), with prefetching, a diagnostics "GLB assets" section, and failure at every level resolved to the existing procedural rendering with the reason reported, never a crash or an empty frame. No legacy asset was converted — the game's geometry was and remains procedural Kotlin, and fabricating files to convert would have broken the no-fabrication rule; .glb is the standard format for all future art. The player character stays procedural (per-frame colour and flag customisation). Registry-backed pieces live in APK assets today (pack-shipped .glb files are the remaining delivery path). 385 passing JVM tests (23 new, over hand-built minimal .glb fixtures: container, geometry, materials, skinning, animation, clip ladder, crossfade, registry failure reporting, save untouched at v8), debug and release builds green on the build runner. Device rendering unverified.
